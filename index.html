The Problem

What if coffee waste wasn't waste?

A city that runs on coffee

Auckland's café culture creates a continuous stream of spent coffee grounds. Once brewed, the grounds are wet, heavy and difficult to handle — and are commonly disposed of as general waste.

Homes that want to grow

At the same time, households, families and schools increasingly want simple, hands-on ways to grow food and experience sustainability — without needing time, space or expertise.

"A waste stream on one side. A food-growing opportunity on the other."

The Transformation

From yesterday's coffee to tomorrow's food.

01
Collect

Spent grounds, saved from the bin

Separated spent coffee grounds are collected from partner cafés across Auckland.

02
Prepare

Prepared & heat-treated

The grounds are mixed into a substrate and heat-treated to create a clean growing medium.

03
Inoculate

Gourmet mushroom spawn

Premium oyster mushroom spawn is introduced into the prepared substrate.

04
Incubate

The mycelium colonises

Inside each kit, the mycelium quietly weaves through the coffee-based substrate.

05
Grow

A daily spray of water

Customers simply mist the kit with water each day and watch the mushrooms emerge.

06
Harvest

Fresh gourmet mushrooms

Fresh gourmet mushrooms, ready to cook in approximately 10–14 days.

07
Return to the Earth

Back into the soil

Spent growing material can be composted — closing the loop and feeding new growth.

Current development formulation

Coffee grounds provide a nutrient-rich input, sawdust contributes structure and carbon, straw improves aeration and bulk, while gypsum supports mineral and pH balance. This is our current working formulation — it will continue to be refined and validated through experimental batches during the pilot.

Innovation Lab

Coffee waste has more lives to give.

TerraCaffe's long-term vision extends beyond mushroom kits. Spent coffee grounds are a rich biomass with potential across materials, packaging and textiles.

Early-stage research

Natural Dyes

Researching the potential to extract natural textile dyes from spent coffee grounds. TerraCaffe has initiated discussions with Dr. Piyal Ariyananda regarding future natural-dye research.

Future application

Biodegradable Paper Products

Potential future applications include coffee cup sleeves, disposable cups, food packaging and paper bags.

Future application

Bio-based Materials

Potential future applications include fibre composites, sustainable packaging and coffee-based biodegradable materials.

These pathways represent future research directions under exploration — not completed research or commercial products.
